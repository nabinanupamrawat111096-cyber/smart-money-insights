Wealth Foundation

The 50/30/20 Budgeting Rule

The foundation of smart money is directing every single rupee with intention. By grouping your expenses, you take control of your financial direction.

Taking Control of Cash Flow

Budgeting is not about limiting your fun; it is about allocating your resources so you can spend guilt-free on what actually brings you value.

Needs: 50% of Income

Essential obligations you cannot skip: rent, mortgage, utilities, basic groceries, transport, health, and minimum debt payments.

Wants: 30% of Income

Flexible spending that enhances life: dining out, shopping, hobbies, vacations, streaming services, and luxury purchases.

Savings & Investments: 20% of Income

Paying your future self: building your emergency fund, stock market investments, retirement accounts, and extra debt payments.

Note: If your current fixed expenses (Needs) exceed 50%, you can use the override section on the calculator to adapt the splits to your reality.

Financial Cushion

Emergency Fund Planning

Before investing or purchasing assets, you must build defense. An emergency fund is your financial shield against job loss, medical emergencies, or car repairs.

Building Financial Security

An emergency fund isn't meant to make you rich; it is meant to keep you secure so that if bad luck strikes, you don't have to borrow high-interest loans.

How many months of expenses?

For most professionals, 3 to 6 months of basic living expenses is perfect. If your income fluctuates (like freelancers or business owners), aim for 9 to 12 months.

Where to keep the fund?

In highly liquid, safe accounts: standard savings accounts, high-yield savings accounts, or short-term fixed deposits. Avoid putting it in volatile stocks.

What is a true emergency?

Job loss, medical urgencies, repair of essential appliances. A retail discount sale is NOT an emergency!

Wealth Acceleration

Compound Interest Simulator

Compounding is the eighth wonder of the world. Compound interest transforms small monthly savings into long-term wealth, giving your money the power to multiply.

The Mathematics of Compound Growth

When you invest, you earn returns. In the next period, you earn returns on your original principal and on the returns you previously earned. That is compound interest.

Start Early (Time is Leverage)

The time you spend in the market is far more critical than timing the market. Starting just 5 years earlier can double your eventual portfolio value.

Consistency Wins

A small monthly contribution (SIP) is easier to maintain and lets you take advantage of dollar-cost averaging during market drops.

Expected Returns

A standard global index fund yields roughly 8-10% long-term. Local indexes (such as NEPSE index) often display higher volatility but can fluctuate near 12-15% over decades.

Financial Freedom

Debt Payoff Acceleration

Debt is a drag on your future income. Eradicating debt using systematic frameworks frees your cash flow, allowing you to invest and grow wealth.

Avalanche vs. Snowball Method

Choosing a debt reduction plan depends heavily on your psychology and cash flow limits.

Debt Avalanche (Mathematically Optimal)

List debts from highest interest rate to lowest interest rate. Pay minimums on all, and dump every extra rupee into the highest interest debt. Saves the most interest.

Debt Snowball (Psychological Wins)

List debts from smallest balance to largest balance. Pay minimums on all, and focus extra cash on wiping out the smallest balance first. Provides quick wins that keep you motivated.

Avoid High-Interest Traps

Pay off high-interest credit cards (18%+ interest) and short-term loans before investing. Wiping out a 15% debt is mathematically equivalent to earning a guaranteed 15% return!
